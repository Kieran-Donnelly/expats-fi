## Verified details

- Basic hook-and-line fishing with one simple pole, hook and bait, but without a spinning reel or lure, is normally a general fishing right and does not require a permit.
- People aged 18–69 normally need the fisheries management fee when fishing with a lure, traps or crayfishing equipment. A grandfathered exemption applies to people who had turned 65 by 31 December 2023.
- The 2026 fisheries management fee costs €6 for one day, €16 for seven days and €47 for the calendar year.
- Fishing with several rods, nets or traps, and fishing at managed destinations, can require the water owner’s permission in addition to the national fee.
- Local restrictions can still apply. Fishers should check the exact water on Kalastusrajoitus.fi and read signs at the location.
- Åland has its own fishing rules and permits.

## Caption

Can you just grab a rod and go fishing in Finland? Sometimes, yes. Sometimes, that very peaceful afternoon can also come with a €100 lesson 🎣

The quick version:

One simple pole with a hook, float and bait, but no spinning reel or lure, is normally free to use.

If you are aged 18–69 and using a spinning rod or another lure setup, you will usually need Finland’s fisheries management fee. In 2026 that costs €6 for one day, €16 for seven days or €47 for the calendar year.

Several rods, nets, traps, crayfishing equipment and certain managed fishing spots can also require permission from the owner of the water. That is separate from the national fee.

The important bit is that a paid fee does not let you fish absolutely everywhere. Check the exact water on Kalastusrajoitus.fi before setting off, and read any signs when you arrive.
